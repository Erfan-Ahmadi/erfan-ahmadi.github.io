When I first wanted to learn how clouds are rendered, the first thing I ran into was a formula with an integral inside an exponent, inside another integral. Every resource I found handed it to me and wanted me to accept it. Nobody explained why there is an $e$ in it at all.

This video isn't a shader tutorial, and it isn't a physics lecture either. It's the part in between. With a laser, a sensor and a box of fog, it builds the absorption term $e^{-\int \sigma\,dx}$ from scratch: why two boxes that each take half the light leave a quarter of it and not nothing, why that turns into an exponential, and why the density ends up as an integral in the exponent.

The exponential turns out not to be a law of nature. It rests on an assumption about how the particles are placed, and that is the one in the title.

Scattering and emission are for the next video.
