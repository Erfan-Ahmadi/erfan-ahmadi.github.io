DXC has two built-in ways to use a buffer device address: `vk::RawBufferLoad` / `vk::RawBufferStore`, and `vk::BufferPointer`. This part shows what each one can do with atomics, and how to combine them with your own SPIR-V functions when they can't.

## vk::RawBufferLoad and vk::RawBufferStore

`vk::RawBufferLoad<T>(address)` reads a `T` at a `uint64_t` address, and `vk::RawBufferStore<T>(address, value)` writes one. An optional last argument sets the alignment; it's 4 by default.

<HlslSpirv html={rawBuffer} />

Each call turns the address into a pointer, uses it once, and drops it. You only ever get a value back, so there's nothing to give an atomic. DXC still accepts this:

<HlslSpirv html={rawBufferErrors} />

The atomic gets the loaded value, `%24 = OpLoad %uint %23 Aligned 4`, instead of a pointer. The validator catches it in DXC's raw output; with optimizations on, the DXC build I tested crashed instead.

## vk::BufferPointer

`vk::BufferPointer<T, Alignment>` is a typed physical pointer built into DXC ([proposal 0010](https://github.com/microsoft/hlsl-specs/blob/main/proposals/0010-vk-buffer-ref.md)). You make one from a `uint64_t` address, and `Get()` returns the `T` it points at. `Get()` is an lvalue: you can read it, write it, and pick members of it.

<HlslSpirv html={bufferPointer} />

`p.Get().mass` became an `OpAccessChain`: a pointer to the member, the same kind of pointer `vk::ext_reference` passes. The next section builds on that.

There's no pointer arithmetic. Do the math on the address and make a new pointer, as above:

<HlslSpirv html={bufferPointerErrors} />

Math on a `uint64_t` needs the `Int64` capability, and `shaderInt64` on the device. To avoid it, keep the address as a `uint32_t2` and use the `bitcast` from Part 2. The output then has no `Int64`:

<HlslSpirv html={bufferPointerErrors2} />

One more difference from C++: buffer pointers are *restrict* by default, so the compiler may assume no two of them point at the same memory. Mark a pointer `[[vk::aliased_pointer]]` when they might.

## vk::BufferPointer with Interlocked*

Since `Get()` is an lvalue, you can pass it to `InterlockedAdd` and friends. You can also pass it to your own function with a `[[vk::ext_reference]]` parameter, like `atomicStore` from Part 1. Here are both:

<HlslSpirv html={bpAtomics} />

On a buffer pointer, `InterlockedAdd` always uses Device scope and relaxed ordering. With your own function you pick both, here a release store. That's the second reason from Part 1, done with nothing more than `vk::ext_reference`.

## Bitcasting vk::BufferPointer for your own SPIR-V functions

`vk::ext_reference` covers most cases. Sometimes you want the pointer itself as a value: to keep it in a struct, or to pass it to a pointer-form function like the `bda_ptr` ones from Part 2. A `vk::BufferPointer` is a physical pointer underneath, so `OpBitcast` turns it into your own pointer type:

Be careful with structs. If your own pointer type points at a struct, DXC doesn't lay that struct out:
